# SettleBack

**Recovery for paid agent jobs.**

An agent pays another agent's service for a job. The request times out. Did the job fail, did only the response get lost, or is the payment still confirming? Today the buyer pays again or writes it off, and the seller answers by reading logs.

SettleBack reconciles the payment, execution, and delivery records of a paid job and takes one bounded step:

| What the records show | What SettleBack does |
|---|---|
| The result exists; only the response was lost | Delivers the existing result to the buyer who paid, checked against its digest, without running or charging the job again. |
| The job failed before producing a result, and the service terms allow repayment | The seller approves in its own wallet and pays a separate repayment to the buyer, signed as an AP2 Payment Mandate carrying an EIP-3009 authorization. |
| The records disagree, or the payment is uncertain | Keeps the case open, freezes further spending, and leaves it to a person. |

Deterministic rules settle the clear cases. When they abstain, Claude reads the logs and the terms and returns cited findings; code checks every quote and any artifact the model points to. Model output never authorizes a transfer. Each recovery is registered, reserved, and recorded on the `RecoveryRegistry` contract on HSK Chain, so both parties can verify the outcome and a payment reference can be used only once.

## Architecture and trust boundaries

```
buyer wallet ──┐                         ┌── HSP gateway (simulated) ── payment chain (simulated)
seller wallet ─┼── case workspace ── SettleBack server ── RecoveryRegistry on HSK Chain
operator ──────┘    (React, browser)     │  (Fastify, SQLite)
                                         └── Claude (assessment only, no tools)
```

- The server decides case state. The browser displays it and asks wallets to sign; nothing a request says can grant a role.
- Buyer and seller keys stay in their wallets. The server holds only the verifier key, which registers cases, reserves retrievals, and records outcomes; it cannot reserve a repayment, act for a seller, or move funds.
- A payment counts as settled only when the gateway record and the on-chain transfer agree. Contradictions freeze the payment for a person.
- The model has no tools and authorizes nothing. Its quotes are matched byte for byte in the stored evidence, and an artifact it names must be confirmed by the job service and the artifact store.
- The registry holds no tokens and has no owner or upgrade path. The verifier is trusted to record truthfully; reservations by the seller are confirmed from the chain.

"Buy a job" starts a new operation from the signed-in wallet. The buyer picks a demo scenario first (the response is lost after the job completes, the job fails, the job finishes after the response deadline, or it completes normally); it configures the faults the demo's job service runs with and is shown as a badge, but the case only ever receives the records the components actually write. The seller's receiving agent quotes with a merchant-signed Cart Mandate, and the buyer's Payment Agent shows every check it made against the pinned merchant key, the order, and the demo buyer's spending limits: passed, blocked with its reason, or not checked because an earlier failure made it moot. A blocked check stops the purchase before anything is signed. The buyer's wallet then signs the EIP-3009 authorization inside an AP2 Payment Mandate, once, with the same idempotency rules as a repayment; the gateway simulator executes it, and the job starts only after reconciliation verifies the payment against the gateway and its chain. An unknown payment never starts the job. When the job answers with an error, or its response deadline (12 seconds) passes, the gateway record and the components' records are stored and one case opens for the operation; records written later, such as a job finishing after the deadline, reach the same case. The page follows the server's state, survives a refresh, and links to the case.

The workspace signs in with the browser wallet and lists only the cases the account is party to (every case for an operator). Each case keeps the payment, execution, and delivery records in separate lanes. It shows what the deterministic rules found, with their quotes, and a cited assessment when the rules abstain. It also shows the one recovery the case may have: its fixed terms, where it stands, and the next step for the signed-in party. The seller approves a per-case repayment by sending the registry reservation from its own wallet and then pays it by signing the repayment's EIP-3009 authorization in the same wallet. The buyer downloads the recovered result, sees the digest the browser computed beside the expected one, and can acknowledge only a match. A state shows as done only once the chain and the registry confirm it. Changing the wallet's account signs the user out. The buyer sees evidence digests and the rules' quotes from the bound terms, but not the seller's records.

## How it works

The demo service performs the purchased work for real: it extracts structured items from the source document and stores the result by its SHA-256 digest. The edge writes nginx-style access lines, the worker writes its own application log under its own job alias and clock, and the job service keeps a structured status whose every change goes through the job state machine. A job starts at most once per operation and is never restarted after an uncertain run. Faults are injected as component behavior (a dropped response, a terminal failure, an edge timeout while the worker finishes late, a lost status update, a corrupted artifact), so disagreeing records come from components that actually disagree.

All fixture data is synthetic. The recovery cases describe what each component must record and the expected outcome; the evidence itself is produced by running the demo service with injected faults, not written by hand. The only exception is buyer claims, which are labeled as hand-written.

Each environment uses its own SQLite database (default `data/settleback-<environment>.db`, ignored by git). A database records the environment it was created for and cannot be opened under another one. The schema enforces the core money rules itself: amounts are positive integer strings in token base units, recorded payment terms and transaction hashes cannot change, each settlement proof can be bound only once, and every update to a mutable record advances its revision by exactly one.

Each operation has at most one case, however many times or by whom it is opened; only the operation's buyer or an operator can open it. Evidence is recorded as immutable items, and its trust level comes from who submitted it, never from what it says: records from the configured service identity are authenticated service records (still the seller's own assertions), gateway and chain records come from server-side reconciliation, and anything a person types or pastes is a claim, even when it looks like a worker log. The bound service terms are always the first item, so their clauses can be cited like any other record. Only plain text and JSON are accepted: at most 20 items, 256 KiB each, and 1 MiB per case, with claims limited to half of each budget so they cannot crowd out service records. Control characters and malformed text are refused, HTML and scripts are stored as inert text, and credentials (private keys, bearer tokens, cookies, API keys, and secret-named fields) are redacted before anything is stored or shown; the digest of the submitted bytes is kept alongside the digest of the stored text. Redaction is pattern-based, not a guarantee. Resubmitting an event with different content is refused and logged rather than overwriting it, and every new item advances the case's evidence revision, which sends a case that is still under review back for reassessment and makes any earlier approval stale.

A rules-only assessment runs before any model is involved. It reads only structured records from trusted sources (the payment attempt, the job service's status, the artifact store, and gateway records) and never free-text logs or claims. It resolves straightforward cases: a completed artifact that matches its digest and its job can be retrieved, a terminal failure before any artifact is repayable under delivery-based terms, started compute stays billable under compute-billable terms, a verified payment with no job can be fulfilled once, and a payment that never produced a transaction needs no action. Contradicting records, such as an artifact whose bytes no longer match, are escalated. When the records do not settle the case, for example when the job's last structured status is still running while its own log says it finished, the rules abstain and say what evidence is missing; a missing record is never read as proof that a job did not run. Every finding cites exact byte ranges of the stored evidence, and the citations are checked the same way any later model citation will be. The fixture cases run end to end against this path: their evidence comes from running the demo service and the gateway simulator, not from hand-written logs.

When the rules abstain, the seller or an operator can request a Claude assessment of that evidence revision. Claude (pinned to `claude-opus-5-5` by default, structured output, no tools) reads the free-text records and the terms and returns findings with verbatim quotes, a suggested action, and the evidence that is missing. Evidence is passed as data inside delimiters the evidence cannot close, and nothing the model returns is trusted as is. The server finds every quote in the item it names and stores the byte range it found, rejects the whole response if any quote is absent or a claim is presented as proof, and records a refusal, a truncated or malformed response, a timeout, or an answer from a different model as a failed assessment that leaves the case with a person. There is no automatic retry and no fallback model; a person can retry a failed attempt, and a successful one is reused for its revision. A suggested repayment is never eligible on the model's word. A suggested retrieval becomes eligible only after code looks up the artifact digest the model quoted from an authenticated record and confirms with the artifact store and the job service that the artifact exists unaltered and was written by this operation's job; a log line naming a digest is a lead, not proof. Every request and result is stored append-only with its prompt digest, model, origin (live, recorded, or scripted test input), raw response, token usage, and duration.

The [evaluation](evaluation/README.md) runs the pinned model over every fixture case: three times through the product path where the rules abstain, once as a probe where they resolve. In the recorded runs, the hidden completion became retrievable in every run only after code verified the artifact the model cited, the same log line without a stored artifact never led to retrieval, and no response failed validation. The raw responses are committed, and a test replays each one through the same validation and decision code. The cases are synthetic and few; this checks specific behaviors and is not an accuracy estimate.

State changes for payments, jobs, cases, and recovery actions go through explicit state machines. Each transition lists who may make it and whether it must cite recorded evidence. The language model is not an actor on any transition, only server-side reconciliation can mark a payment verified, and an unknown payment can never be released for a fresh attempt automatically.

The local environment runs against a simulator for the HSP gateway and its chain, and against the real registry contract on a disposable local chain. The gateway simulator follows the documented statuses and webhook retries and can inject lost responses, failed broadcasts, reorganizations, delayed finality, wrong receipts, missing transactions, outages, and unreachable webhooks. Everything produced locally, including registry records on the disposable chain, is labeled simulated and refused outside the local environment. Every API response carries `x-settleback-mode` and `x-settleback-network` headers, and the interface shows when it is a simulation. A payment counts as settled only when the gateway record and the on-chain transfer both match the expected payer, payee, token, chain, and principal amount.

Payments follow the HSP Merchant gateway's agent-to-agent pattern as its public documentation describes it. The receiving agent builds a Cart Mandate for the job's fixed terms (one x402 option on the operation's chain and token, paid to the seller, for exactly the job's USD price), hashes the canonical JSON of its contents, signs that hash in an ES256K merchant JWT, and creates the order with an HMAC-signed request. The attempt and its gateway identifiers are stored before the order is created, so a lost response or a restart is recovered by querying the same identifiers and never opens a second order. Before anything is signed, the buyer's Payment Agent recomputes the cart hash over the contents it received, verifies the JWT against the merchant key it pinned beforehand (algorithm, audience, issuer, expiry), checks the cart's expiry and the gateway's amount rules, and requires the payee, chain, token, and USD amount due to match the operation, the payee to be on its allowlist, and the price plus the maximum fee to fit its per-payment and daily limits. The Payment Mandate is built only from that validated cart, is signed once per attempt, and after a lost submission the outcome is read back from the gateway instead of signing again. The gateway simulator applies the same request-signature, nonce, JWT, cart-hash, amount, expiry, and one-time-cart checks, and accepts a mandate only when it is bound to the order's cart hash and amount due. The gateway documentation describes no Payment Mandate submission endpoint. The merchant SDKs published alongside it ([hashkey-merchant/sdks](https://github.com/hashkey-merchant/sdks)) define the AP2 Payment Mandate and the payer's EIP-712 token authorizations, which repayments use (below); the seeded cases' original payments carry a labeled placeholder signature, while a job bought from the workspace is paid with the buyer's wallet signature in the same AP2 form. Live completion waits for the submission endpoint and gateway access. A gateway-reported USD rate other than the pinned stablecoin rate blocks verification.

A payment attempt is executed only after its one execution permit is consumed, in the same database transaction that records it as signing-requested together with the digest of the Payment Mandate about to be signed. Only the caller that consumed the permit signs and submits, once: twenty concurrent requests, including ones from separate database connections, produce one signature and one submission. A permit is never reissued. After a lost response, a signer failure, or a crash, the attempt stays signing-requested, and no other attempt for the same payment can start while it is live, so an ambiguous payment freezes instead of being sent twice. Reconciliation only observes and is safe to repeat. It moves an attempt forward only as far as the gateway record and the on-chain transfer together support, and it records a payment as verified only at the required gateway level with a matching transfer. A contradiction makes the attempt unknown for an operator to close with evidence: a mismatched or missing transfer, a failure after inclusion, a changed gateway flow, or a payment that disappears. An unreachable gateway changes nothing. The server runs this pass every 15 seconds, and the same pass retries registry outcomes that could not be written, such as an acknowledged retrieval. Outside the local demo no payment rail is configured yet, so there the passes only retry registry outcomes.

The seller pays a reserved per-case repayment as a new agent-to-agent payment in the opposite direction: from the seller to the buyer who paid, on the original chain and token, for the principal. It is never a reversal of the original payment. Before anything is created, the reservation must be current both in the database and on the registry. The repayment attempt and its gateway identifiers are stored before the order is created. The seller's Payment Agent accepts the Cart Mandate only if it pays exactly that transfer. The deadline and the evidence are checked again inside the transaction that consumes the execution permit and marks the repayment as requested, so a reservation that expires or goes stale in the meantime is never paid. However often the seller asks, one transfer is made. The case closes only when two things hold. First, the transfer is verified final on chain and bound to this case as its repayment proof; a transaction already bound anywhere else is refused. Second, the verifier's REPAID record is confirmed on the registry, with the transaction and a digest of the verified evidence. A verified repayment whose registry record has not landed stays record-pending, and the next reconciliation pass retries the record, never the payment. Standing-policy repayments wait for the seller's guarded signer. In local development the repayment runs through the gateway simulator and is labeled as simulated. The simulator draws its transaction hashes from a random seed, so a restart never reuses a payment reference the registry already holds.

A seller paying from its own wallet signs the repayment in the form the merchant SDKs give the AP2 Payment Mandate: `payment_mandate_contents` with the payment request as its ID and an x402 v2 `exact` payment response that carries an EIP-3009 `TransferWithAuthorization` of exactly the repayment, from the seller to the buyer who paid, for the principal, valid until the repayment's cart expires. The token's EIP-712 domain is pinned per chain and token, never taken from a request; for the HSK Chain testnet USDC a test pins it to the domain separator and type hash read from the token contract. The authorization's nonce is derived from the order and the payer, so an attempt always yields the same authorization and the token can execute it at most once, however often it is signed or submitted. Requesting the repayment consumes the execution permit and records the mandate's digest. The server returns the typed data for `eth_signTypedData_v4` and, until a signature arrives, offers the same mandate again after checking it against that digest. A signature is accepted only if it recovers to the authorization's `from` over exactly the recorded mandate; it is then submitted once, and a repeat after a lost response is answered from the gateway. An attempt that reconciliation has frozen is not submitted again. The gateway simulator checks what the token contract and the gateway would: the pinned domain, the signer, recipient, value, validity window, an unused nonce, and the mandate's binding to its order. The signature is real; the gateway that would execute it is simulated until its submission endpoint is published and access is granted.

The `RecoveryRegistry` contract holds no tokens, makes no external calls, and has no owner, upgrade path, case cancellation, budget reset, or reservation release. A verifier address fixed at deployment registers each case with its terms (buyer, seller, token, principal, the original payment's chain and transaction, the service-terms and eligibility-rules hashes, the action and evidence digests, the action kind, and a deadline) and later records the single outcome. Every payment reference is qualified by its chain and can be bound only once, so an original payment can never be presented as a repayment or reused for another case. A repayment is reserved either by the seller for that one case or by the seller's delegate under a standing policy the seller activated. A policy is an immutable version bound to the delegate, signer, token, payment chain, service-terms hash, eligibility-rules hash, per-case and daily limits, and a validity window; only the seller can activate, pause, resume, revoke, or replace it, and one version is active per seller, chain, and token. Exposure belongs to that scope, not to a version: every reservation, including explicit per-case ones, stays outstanding across days and versions until the verifier records the repayment, which moves it to the day's booked total in one step. An automatic reservation must fit `booked today + outstanding + amount <= daily limit` and the per-case limit, so pausing, revoking, replacing, or waiting never frees uncertain budget. Retrieval of an existing result is reserved and completed by the verifier and involves no money. The verifier is trusted to record truthfully; it cannot reserve a repayment, act for a seller, or change a policy. A case gets at most one recovery action, and it is fixed before anyone approves it. Eligibility is recomputed from the current evidence: a repayment only when the rules find a refundable failure, a retrieval when the rules find a retrievable artifact or when an assessment of this evidence revision verified one. The proposal binds the action, its basis, the evidence revision and digest, the service terms, the original payment, the exact repayment (from the seller to the buyer who paid, on the original chain and token, for the principal) or the artifact to deliver, a deadline, the registry, and the authority mode; nothing in it comes from the requester. The verifier registers the case with the proposal's digest. A retrieval is then reserved by the verifier. A repayment goes to the seller's delegate when the seller's active standing policy covers it (status, terms, rules, validity, per-case and daily limits read from the registry), and otherwise to the seller for an explicit reservation from their own wallet. A reported reservation counts only after the chain confirms it and the registry's case terms equal the proposal. A reservation recorded after the evidence changed or after the deadline is kept, because the chain holds it, but it can never execute; an operator can strand it, and nothing resets it or creates a second action.

A reserved retrieval is delivered only to the operation's buyer, from the artifact store, without running the job again or charging anything. Before any bytes are released, the job service and the artifact store must confirm, with the same checks that make an artifact lead eligible, that this operation's job wrote the artifact and that it is unaltered, and the bytes must hash to the digest the proposal fixed; the first release also needs a current reservation that the registry still holds. A failed check leaves the delivery pending. The case is recovered only after the buyer acknowledges the expected digest. The acknowledgement is stored once per operation, and the verifier records the artifact digest and the acknowledgement's digest as the registry outcome. A lost response, a repeated acknowledgement, or a registry write whose receipt was lost is resolved from what is stored and what the chain holds, never by running the work again. The acknowledgement is an authenticated statement of receipt, not proof that the buyer is satisfied.

People sign in with their wallet (EIP-4361). The server issues a short-lived message naming the SettleBack web origin, the network profile, and the chain, and stating that signing authorizes no payment. The challenge is not stored: its fields travel under the server's MAC, which is also the nonce, so requests from people who have not signed in write nothing. The server rebuilds the message from those fields and accepts only an EOA signature over it; contract wallets cannot sign in yet. A challenge expires after five minutes and opens at most one session, even when presented concurrently. The session is an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie (`__Host-` prefixed and Secure over HTTPS) that lasts two hours, and only its token's SHA-256 is stored. Sessions are revoked on logout or a new sign-in in the same browser, never deleted. A write from any origin other than the configured web origin is refused before its body is read, and JSON is the only body type accepted, so a cross-site form cannot submit one. A request that changes state must also name the wallet the browser is connected to; a different account, or a chain the environment does not use, ends the session. Roles come only from the session and the operation: the operation's buyer and seller, and operator addresses set in configuration (a session loses that role when the configuration drops it). Nothing in a request can claim a role. An account with no relation to an operation gets the same not-found answer as an unknown ID. The buyer sees evidence digests but not the seller's records or the model's quotes from them. Delivered results are served as opaque downloads, never rendered by the application.

The application talks to the registry through one adapter, whether the contract runs on HSK Chain or, for local work, on a disposable chain such as anvil; there is no separate registry simulator to drift from the contract. On connection it checks the chain ID, that the address holds code, and that its verifier key is the contract's verifier. Verifier writes are simulated first, so a refusal is reported by the contract's error name without spending gas, and a sent write whose receipt does not arrive is reported with its hash as an unknown outcome. Public RPC endpoints balance requests across nodes that may be a block or two behind, so the adapter reads its own writes: after a write or a confirmed reservation, reads, simulations, and gas estimates are pinned to a block at least that recent, and a node that has not caught up is asked again until the timeout. The transaction is sent with its gas, fees, and nonce filled in, so nothing is re-estimated against a lagging node; a nonce that node reported too low is retried with the next one, at most three times. A reservation reported the moment it is sent is looked up a few times before it is called unknown to the chain. Seller and delegate actions are never signed by the application: it prepares the call for their wallet and then confirms the reported transaction from the chain, accepting it only as a successful call to this registry from the expected account that emitted exactly one reservation event for that case, in the expected mode and authorization, with enough confirmations, while the case still records that reservation. A transaction that is not yet mined stays pending. A separate `SettleBackDeploymentMarker` records, on another network, the commit and the chain and address of the registry deployment it points to; it is a pointer, not the registry.

`SETTLEBACK_ENV` selects `local`, `testnet`, `mixed` (testnet payments with the mainnet registry), or `mainnet`. Payment writes are disabled by default, and no repayment is paid unless they are enabled, whatever rail is connected. Live environments refuse to enable them until their chain, token, gateway, and finality settings exist. Live environments also need `SETTLEBACK_WEB_ORIGIN`, their HTTPS web origin; operators are listed in `SETTLEBACK_OPERATORS`.
